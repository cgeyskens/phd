Possible standard tags: P17, CUSF, FH8, TRX, ZZ, SUMO, SNUT
- P17: very small, hydrophilic; boosts solubility + thermostability 
- FH8: small, very soluble/thermostable; can also act as purification handle 
- SUMO (HSUMO3): “detergent-like” effect on otherwise insoluble proteins 
- TRX: classic solubility enhancer for heterologous proteins 
- SNUT: solubility-enhancing “unique tag” derived from sortase; intended to help difficult soluble domains 
- CUSF: beta-barrel tag; proposed solubility/periplasmic fusion behavior 
- ZZ: Protein A IgG-repeat domain (often used as fusion/handle) 

Literature Research:
- Do any of these proteins have crystal structures/cryoEM? Look at how they produced the proteins.
- Do any of these proteins have been expressed or purified for other experiments? Look at how they produced the proteins.
- Or do they have any trouble in general to producing them?
- Any aggregation prone or unstable?

### ========================= PMCH_P56942 ========================= ###
Best tag: P17 (or FH8)
Why: It’s a small secretory peptide precursor; you usually want the lightest solubility help and easiest downstream cleavage/handling. 
There are recombinant PMCH products made in E. coli with His-tag, suggesting it’s not inherently “impossible,” but processing/folding can still be annoying. 

Trouble risk: moderate (small peptide, disulfide requirements depending on what fragment you express).
Flag: Try 2 tags → P17 + FH8.


### ========================= CHGB_P16014 ========================= ###
Best tag: SUMO (backup: SNUT)
Why: CHGB is a big secretory/granule protein and is reported to partition into soluble and membrane-associated forms, 
and some workflows use detergents for extraction—signals “sticky / aggregation-prone behavior”. 

Also, recombinant CHGB fragments have been produced in E. coli (His-tag), which suggests truncations are doable but may need help staying soluble. 

Trouble risk: high (size + stickiness).
Flag: Try 2 tags → SUMO + SNUT.

Studies where they purified the protein:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37435575/


### ========================= NRN1_Q8CFV4 ========================= ###
Best tag: P17 (backup: TRX or FH8)
Why: Neuritin has been expressed recombinantly in E. coli in published work. 

This is a “secreted/GPI-related” protein class that often benefits from mild solubility help and good redox 
(which you’re already handling with additives).
Trouble risk: medium (disulfides).
Flag: Try 2 tags → P17 + TRX.

Studies where they produced the protein:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26751893/


### ========================= NTNG1_Q8R4G0 ========================= ###
Best tag: FH8 (backup: P17)
Why: Commercial recombinant NTNG1 is commonly produced in mammalian cells with a C-terminal His tag, which is typical 
for disulfide-rich extracellular guidance cues. 

FH8 is small, very soluble, and often a good choice for extracellular/disulfide-rich domains when you still want a 
compact fusion. 

Trouble risk: high-ish (extracellular disulfide folding).
Flag: Try 2 tags → FH8 + P17.

Protein production paper:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/emboj.2011.346#Sec8


### ========================= RTN4R_Q99PI8 ========================= ###
Best tag: FH8 (backup: SUMO)
Why: There are structures of RTN4R ectodomain; at least one PDB entry reports expression in insect cells (Trichoplusia ni), again typical for disulfide-rich extracellular proteins. 
ebi.ac.uk

If you’re expressing it in a soluble cell-free workflow, you’ll likely need a strong “keep-it-soluble” fusion—FH8 is a great first pick; SUMO is the “if it’s still sticky” backup. 
Nuclera

Trouble risk: high (LRR ectodomain + disulfide caps → aggregation if misfolded).
Flag: Try 2 tags → FH8 + SUMO.

Studies where they did Cryo-EM:

### ========================= LY6H_Q9WUC3 ========================= ###
Best tag: TRX (backup: FH8)
Why: LY6-family/LU-domain proteins are typically very disulfide rich and can be hard to keep soluble unless folding is supported; 
TRX is a classic solubility enhancer and often pairs well with oxidative folding conditions. 

Trouble risk: high (disulfide density).
Flag: Try 2 tags → TRX + FH8.


### ========================= ITM2C_Q91VK4_extracel ========================= ###
Best tag: P17 (backup: FH8)
Why: BRICHOS domains are soluble-ish domains, but BRI3 BRICHOS forms larger oligomers and is discussed in the context of 
preventing aggregation—i.e., it has “self-association/oligomer” behavior that can complicate purification. 

Use small tags first to avoid pushing oligomeric behavior further. 

Trouble risk: medium–high (oligomerization).
Flag: Try 2 tags → P17 + FH8.

Studies where they produced a large domain of the protein:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32555390/


### ========================= ADGRB3_Q80ZF8_extracel ========================= ###
Best tag: SNUT (backup: SUMO)
Why: This is a very large, multi-domain extracellular region (CUB, TSR repeats, GAIN/GPS typical of adhesion GPCRs), 
which is a classic “difficult soluble recombinant” category. 

For “hard mode” domains, SNUT/SUMO are your best bets in this tag set. 

Trouble risk: very high (size, disulfides, domain boundaries).
Flag: Try 2 tags → SNUT + SUMO.

Protein production paper of family protein:
https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2023/03/15/2023.03.14.532526.full.pdf


### ========================= ITM2B_O89051_extracel ========================= ###
Best tag: P17 (backup: FH8)
Why: The Bri2 BRICHOS domain has cryoEM structure (e.g., PDB 8RNU), meaning soluble recombinant protein was produced 
successfully for structural work. However, BRICHOS systems can oligomerize; start small and soluble. 

Trouble risk: medium (oligomerization/aggregation interactions).
Flag: Try 2 tags → P17 + FH8.

Studies where they produced a large domain of the protein:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32555390/


### ======= CDH11_P55288_extracel, CDH10_P70408_extracel, CDH6_P97326_extracel, CDH4_P39038_extracel ========= ###
Best tag: P17 (backup: FH8)
Why: Cadherin ectodomains are usually produced in mammalian systems (often with Fc/His formats) and 
are Ca²⁺-dependent for stability. You generally don’t want a bulky tag that might interfere with 
cadherin EC-repeat behavior; small tags are safer. 

Trouble risk: medium–high (needs Ca²⁺; can be floppy/aggregation-prone without it).
Flag: Try 2 tags → P17 + FH8 (but keep CaCl₂ in your additive conditions for this one).

Studies where they produced a large domain of the protein:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38205802/
https://www.cell.com/structure/fulltext/S0969-2126%2821%2900210-0

Production pattern:
- Express soluble ectodomain or EC1–2
- Use mammalian or insect cells for correct folding/PTMs when needed
- Maintain Ca²⁺ during purification/assays because cadherins are Ca²⁺-depend
